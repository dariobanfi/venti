france.geojson in this folder is the outline of metropolitan France (the
mainland, its islands and Corsica), made from data of IGN, the Institut
national de l'information géographique et forestière: ADMIN EXPRESS COG,
edition 2026, the layer ADMINEXPRESS-COG.2026:region of the Géoplateforme's
WFS, under the Licence Ouverte / Open Licence 2.0 (Etalab). It is not
covered by the licence of the code around it.

Credit, in our own words until IGN's own wording is found (to verify):
"IGN – ADMIN EXPRESS COG 2026, Licence Ouverte; régions réunies et
simplifiées", with
https://cartes.gouv.fr/rechercher-une-donnee/dataset/IGNF_ADMIN-EXPRESS as
its link. The licence asks for the source and the date of its last update:
the record of the data set was last revised on 2026-11-19, and the regions
were fetched on 2026-11-22.

What was done to it: the thirteen metropolitan regions (3,318 rings,
3,226,785 positions, in WGS 84 as the service gives them) were joined into
one outline by dropping every piece of boundary that two regions share;
each ring was simplified by Douglas-Peucker in Lambert-93 metres, at 5 m
east of 5.5° E between 43.6° and 47.7° N, where Switzerland, Italy and
Monaco are, and at 15 m elsewhere; the 2,991 islets under a hectare were
dropped; positions were rounded to six decimals. 310 rings: the mainland
(83,306 positions), Corsica (14,061), 307 islands, and one hole of 13 km²,
the Spanish town of Llívia. 112,398 positions, 2,385,264 bytes, 548,856 km²
in Lambert-93. ../tools/build_outline.py makes it again, with Python 3 and
nothing else.

Two things to know. At the sea and on Lake Geneva the outline is the
shore: the regions hold no water. And on Mont Blanc it is the border as
France draws it, which Italy draws differently around the summit: the
summit lies about 275 m inside the outline, and the outline passes by Mont
Blanc de Courmayeur.

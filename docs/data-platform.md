## The direction

The owner, 2026-11-21:

> Make sure that when building the server, you have to think about it as a data platform first, because we want this server to become the center point of paragliding, and people can use it to create takeoffs, track their flights, any kind of community data. It will become the central hub for it in the future. That's my vision, at least. So it needs to be very open and supporting of that, and allow people to put their data in there and query it.

It stands beside the decisions that bind it: free and open source (1), one central platform on the owner's own server and nothing that bills by use (5), every external source behind an adapter (6), AGPL-3.0-or-later for the code (12), many users (13).

## Principles

A new endpoint or a new table is judged by these. Each is a question with a yes or a no.

1. **A record, not a screen's state.** Does the table hold the thing itself (a take-off, a flight, a plan), so that a screen nobody has thought of yet could be built from it? An answer may be shaped for a screen only beside an answer that is not.
2. **Author, times, origin.** Does every record a pilot made say who made it, when it was made and when it last changed, and through what it came in (which app or token)? Does a record that others may correct keep every earlier state and who made each change?
3. **A licence on everything handed out.** Does every answer and every file say under which licence its records are given, per source, as `attributions` does today for the data of others (`crates/api-types/src/v1/attribution.rs`)? The community's own data is a source like any other, with a licence of its own.
4. **Others' data and the community's stay apart.** Is the community's data in tables, layers and files of its own, so that it can be handed out whole without a record of another licence family in it? ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md#licence-rules-built-into-the-pipeline))
5. **Who may read is written down, in one place per kind.** Is the visibility of a record a column with named values, held by the database, and the same rule for the API, the connector, a share page and a dump? Today's model for it is `flights.visibility` with its constraint (`crates/store/migrations/0002_accounts_sessions_flights.sql`, `0009_reports_and_takedowns.sql`).
6. **Asked for as a program asks.** Can what is open be asked for by identifier, by place, by time and by pilot, page after page with an opaque cursor, and "what changed since", deletions included? Today's model is `GET /v1/flights/changes` ([api.md](api.md#get-v1flightschangessincelimit)).
7. **Documented is enough.** Can a program that is not Venti's app, written in any language, use it from the documents alone, without the Rust core? Nothing is readable only by `crates/core`.
8. **What a pilot may write, a program may write for them with their leave.** Does the write take a token that the pilot gave to another maker's app, limited to what the pilot allowed and withdrawn by the pilot at any time? It never needs the pilot's password.
9. **An identifier stays.** Is the record's identifier never used for another record, and its address the same for good? A record that is gone answers that it is gone.
10. **On the server unless it is the pilot's private matter.** If it lives on the device alone: is it private, and can the pilot move it to their account when they choose?
11. **Theirs to take and to remove.** Can a pilot get everything they gave in formats other programs read, and remove it? Where a contribution was given under an open licence and stays, do the terms say so before it is given?
12. **Open does not mean unbounded.** Is every read and write bounded per token or per address, and is the bound in the documents?

## What the server holds today

### Data of others, through adapters

One table family per kind in `crates/store/migrations`; the licence is each source's `manifest.toml`, verified as [data-sources.md](data-sources.md) records.

| Kind | Tables | Sources and their licence | How it is kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wind stations and observations | `stations`, `observations` (by month), `source_runs` (`0001`) | DWD and GeoSphere Austria: CC BY 4.0. South Tyrol: CC0 1.0. Airfield reports through the AWC: public domain | A row per station and per reading, each with its source; thirteen months |
| Airspace | `airspace_sets`, `airspaces` (`0003`, `0004`) | openAIP: CC BY-NC 4.0 with a share-alike condition | The latest whole set of each source |
| Flying sites | `site_sets`, `sites` (`0005`) | OpenStreetMap: ODbL 1.0, share-alike | The latest whole set of each source; a site is its record as JSON text with a position (`crates/store/src/site.rs`) |
| Forecasts | `forecast_models` (`0006`); series are fetched on demand and kept in memory | Open-Meteo with DWD's ICON-D2 and ICON-EU: CC BY 4.0 | Not stored beyond half an hour |
| The thermal map | `thermal_map_parts` (`0011`) | Venti's own estimate from the forecast above | Remade with every model run |
| Rain radar | In memory (`crates/server/src/radar.rs`) | DWD: CC BY 4.0 | The newest frames |
| Aerial photographs of Austria | Tiles on disk (`crates/server/src/imagery.rs`) | basemap.at: CC BY 4.0 | Passed on, cut at the border |
| Aerial photographs of France | Tiles on disk (`crates/server/src/imagery.rs`) | IGN, BD ORTHO: Licence Ouverte 2.0 | Passed on, cut at the border, from zoom 13 |
| Map regions | Files in a folder (`crates/server/src/maps.rs`) | OpenStreetMap and the terrain's sources | Handed out as files |

Three licence families are in the store: attribution only (CC BY, CC0, public domain), share-alike (ODbL), and non-commercial (openAIP). They never share a table row, a layer or an answer's record.

**Two kinds of credential since 2026-11-21.** Until then every write, and every read of what is a pilot's own, took the session token of the app's own sign-in and nothing else, so that an app of another maker could act for a pilot only by asking for the pilot's password. A session is still got with the password. The connector takes no token at all.

A named waypoint at a take-off or a landing is the raw material of a pilot-made site: offering "make this a take-off on Venti" is the bridge, and it is the pilot's act, never the server's |
| The task or route | `crates/core/src/task.rs` (one XCTSK file at a time), `crates/core/src/route_plan.rs` | Private by default. Shared routes ("common cross-country lines" in [plan.md](plan.md#today-and-plan)) are community data and want a record of their own on the server; not designed here |
| Airspace files the pilot brought | `crates/core/src/airspace.rs`: OpenAir files as they came | Private, and not the pilot's to publish: the files are somebody else's work under terms the server cannot know. They stay on the device |
| Airspace kept from the server | `crates/core/src/kept_airspace.rs` | A cache of the server's answer. Nothing to move |

## Take-offs and landings made by pilots

Until 2026-11-21 every flying site was OpenStreetMap's, read-only, and thin: 252 take-offs in the first region, 93 of them with wind directions, and well-known mountains missing ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md#flying-sites)). The catalogue there had the row this section fills: "Our own community edits". The subsections after the first are the design as the review wrote it, brought up to date where the piece decided something; the first says what stands.

### What stands of it (2026-11-21)

**Two layers, since the owner's two messages of 2026-11-21** ([plan.md](plan.md), owner decision 14: take-offs "usually are … really official", so the server takes them from "trusted partners"; and beside that "a crowdsourced feature, which is less official … not maybe displayed by default … where someone can say, yes, I started here and it was good"). What follows in this section was written for one layer that any pilot wrote to; it is true of both layers except for who writes, which is now this:

- **`venti`, the partners' layer.** Written by an account whoever runs the server named as trusted with it: one fact on the account (`accounts.edits_sites_since`, migration `0018`), granted, withdrawn and listed from the command line (`grant-sites`, `withdraw-sites`, `site-partners`; [running-a-server.md](running-a-server.md#sites-that-partners-and-pilots-add)), and told to the account's own apps in `GET /v1/account` (`edits_sites`). No setting: the trust is the gate. Withdrawing it leaves what the account wrote. In every answer of `GET /v1/sites`, as before.
- **`venti-pilots`, the crowdsourced layer.** Written by any signed-in pilot whose publishing is not suspended, where `sites_from_pilots` is on; a partner corrects its sites too. In an answer only when the request names it (`layers=default,venti-pilots`).
- **Which layer a site is of follows from who added it**, is the beginning of its identifier, and never changes. A pilot at a partner's site is refused (`403` `sites_for_partners`) and reports it. One table and one count of changes hold both layers; "what changed since" is asked a layer at a time (`layer=`).
- **20 m.** A pilot's site keeps the distance from every site of both layers; a partner's from partners' sites only. A partner's site is therefore put where a pilot's stands, and both stand: moving a crowdsourced site into the partners' layer ("adopt") is not built and is the next step.
- **Confirmations.** "I took off here and it was good": one for an account and a site of either layer, with a day (today or up to 90 days back), an optional line and optionally one of the pilot's own flights, of which the server keeps only that it took off within 500 m of the site. A table of their own (`site_confirmations`), read by anyone; the site's standing says how many pilots and the newest day. A confirmation is no revision and is not given under CC0: it is the pilot's word and goes with the account. A mirror follows them through "what changed since", which hands a site out again when a confirmation of it is given or taken back; one that goes with a deleted account gives the site no new number yet (to do). Not reportable by themselves yet.
- **CC0 1.0 for both layers**: a partner gives the sites they add under it as a pilot does.
- **Names from before.** `pilot_sites`, `PilotSite` and the core's `SiteByPilots` stand for a site of either layer; the core's `SiteByPilots.from_pilots` says which.
- **In the core**: the two notices, the refusal's words, `sites_path_with_pilots` for a pilot who switched the layer on, the line "3 pilots took off here, last on 14 November 2026", and a partner's `giving` sentence. Not in the core yet: whether the signed-in account is a partner (the web reads `edits_sites` from `GET /v1/account` until it is), and everything for confirming (the request, the list's reader, the day's choice, the refusals' words).
- **A server that had the setting on before this**: the sites pilots wrote to `venti` then are partners' sites now. Whoever runs it looks at them (`site`) and takes down what should not stand there.

Piece 1 of [the build order](#what-is-missing-in-build-order), on the server, and of piece 2 the core's part: the apps' core reads the layer, and since 2026-11-21 has everything a screen needs to add and correct a site, to show its history and to report it ([design.md](design.md#map), "Adding and correcting a site"). No app has such a screen yet.

**What was run.** All of it is in the code and its tests ran on 2026-11-21 against the in-memory store: the store's suite (`crates/store/tests/suite/pilot_sites.rs`), the API through the real router with golden answers that [api.md](api.md#sites-that-ventis-partners-and-pilots-add) shows (`crates/server/tests/sites.rs`), and the operator's commands (`crates/server/tests/moderation.rs`). The migration and the queries for PostgreSQL were compiled and not run against a database: *to verify* on the first installation.

- **The record and its history.** A site a pilot made has an identifier of the server's own, `venti:` and 26 characters, that is never used for another site; no row is ever deleted (`crates/store/migrations/0013_pilot_sites.sql`). Every change is a revision that is kept, with the whole record as it stood from then on, its author, its time, through what it came in and a line of up to 200 characters saying why (`crates/store/src/pilot_site.rs`). Both stores decide what a revision comes to in one function, `decide`, in that file.
- **Undo and removal.** Putting a site back to an earlier revision is a new revision that names it: `POST /v1/sites/{id}/revisions` with `{"restore": n}`. Removing is a revision too. The store keeps the two ways a site can be gone apart (`removed_at`, `taken_down`), and holds a site that is taken down against every revision but the operator's. In the API (`crates/server/src/api/sites.rs`): a site a pilot removed answers `410` `site_gone` at its address, keeps a readable history, and comes back when any signed-in pilot restores a revision; a site that whoever runs the server took down (`remove-site`) answers the same at its address, its history is closed as well, and only the operator brings it back (`revert-site`).
- **Reading.** The layer `venti` in `GET /v1/sites`; one site of either layer by its address; a pilot-made site's history; what changed in the layer since a cursor, removals included. Every answer carries the layer's credit and licence in `attributions`: source `venti`, provider "Venti contributors", licence "CC0 1.0".
- **Writing.** Adding, correcting, removing and restoring, with a session token. Who may write is one function, `may_write` in `crates/server/src/pilot_sites.rs`: any signed-in pilot whose publishing is not suspended. A change names the revision it was made from, and is refused with `409` `site_changed` when somebody else changed the site meanwhile.
- **What the server checks.** What every site record passes, the same code as for OpenStreetMap's (`Site::check` in `crates/domain/src/site.rs`, with its numbers as constants of `Site`; `check_site` in `crates/sources/src/sites.rs` calls it, and what a site from a pilot has to be beyond that is `SiteDraft::record` in `crates/api-types/src/v1/sites.rs`, which the server and the apps' core both ask): a name of up to 200 characters, a description of up to 1,000, the words about a fee and about a time without flying up to 100 each, a web address of plain `http` or `https` form up to 500, a height from −500 to 9,000 m, at most 16 wind sectors within 0° to 360°, and nothing that does not print. Beyond that: a name and at least one use; the place inside the bounds of a region the server serves; no standing site of the layer within 20 m (`409` `site_too_close`, whose error names the other one by identifier and name in `error.site`); 20 new sites and 100 revisions a day per account, counted in the process's memory as the other limits are.
- **The licence in one place.** `VENTI_SITES_LICENCE` and its address in `crates/api-types/src/v1/sites.rs`, which `LICENCE` in `crates/server/src/pilot_sites.rs` and the core's form read: CC0 1.0, [the owner's decision](#the-licence-cc0-10-decided-by-the-owner). The source `venti` has no `manifest.toml`: it is no adapter and fetches nothing, so the constant stands where a manifest's licence would.
- **A setting that lets pilots write.** `sites_from_pilots` (`VENTI_SITES_FROM_PILOTS`), off unless set and on in the development mode. With it off every write answers `403` `sites_closed`, and `GET /v1/health` says `"sites_from_pilots": "closed"` so that an app does not offer the form; reading is always on. The reason is [the terms for contributors](#what-the-terms-for-contributors-must-say), which do not exist yet: before a public server takes a site from a pilot, the pilot has to have been told that it is given under CC0 1.0 and stays when the account goes.
- **The operator's commands.** `site` (the record with its history), `revert-site` (restores a revision, also of a site that is taken down) and `remove-site` (takes a site down): [running-a-server.md](running-a-server.md).
- **The development mode.** It serves four made-up sites of its three pilots, with their histories, one of them removed (`DEMO_SITES` in `crates/server/src/dev.rs`).
- **Reports about a site** (2026-11-21). A signed-in pilot reports a site of the layer as it stands, or one revision of it: `POST /v1/reports` with `subject.site`. Such a report names no second account (`reports.about_account_id` may be empty for the kind `site` and for no other, `crates/store/migrations/0015_reports_about_sites.sql`), stays when the site's author goes, and copies nothing of the site: it names the revision, and the operator's `reports` says where the site stands now and how to read it (`venti-server site …`). Two reasons of its own, `wrong_or_dangerous` and `not_a_site`. Run against PostgreSQL on 2026-11-21 with the store's suite.
- **The core writes to the layer** (2026-11-21, `crates/core/src/site_edit.rs`): the draft, the form's words and limits, the check before sending, the requests, the history in words and every refusal in words; the wasm binding carries all of it. Tested against the server's golden answers; on no screen yet.
- **The assistant's tool** `sites_near` marks every site of the partners' layer in its own entry (`added_by_a_partner`: added by a partner of Venti, not checked by Venti) and says in its description that its name is a partner's free text. It does not ask for the places from pilots: the apps show them only when the pilot switches them on, and the tool does not know that switch, as the connector's `sites_in_box` leaves them out unless `places_from_pilots` is asked for. A place from pilots that a server sends all the same is marked `not_official`, in the connector's words (2026-11-21; before, every site of `venti` was marked as the pilots', from before there were two layers).
- **The core reads the layer** through the calls the apps already make (`crates/core/src/sites.rs`, `kept_sites.rs`), so that the three apps show the community's sites beside OpenStreetMap's without a change of their own; no app was built and looked at with it on 2026-11-21, *to verify*. Adding and correcting from the apps is piece 2.

Not built in this piece:

- **Hiding one revision.** A revision whose text should not be read stays in the history of a standing site; the operator can only take the whole site down.
- **The link to an OpenStreetMap element** that is the same place, and a rule for which of two layers a map draws there.
- **Photos**; tokens with scopes (piece 3); a dump of the layer (piece 10); the layer in a pilot's archive (piece 6).
- **An account's deletion takes no change number**: a mirror that follows `GET /v1/sites/changes` does not learn from it that a site lost its author's name.

### The record

The same canonical record the apps already draw, filter and keep, `venti_domain::Site` (`crates/domain/src/site.rs`), so that a pilot-made site needs no second code path in the core's filter (`site_filter`), Today (`kept_sites`) or the tools:

- name; position; height above sea level; uses (take-off, landing, winch, training); wind directions as sectors; whether for paragliders and for hang gliders; a web page; a description; whether a fee is asked; whether guests have rules to read; a time without flying; an outline for a landing field.
- Not taken from pilots as a plain field: `official`. Who says a site is approved is a question of authority (a club, the DHV), not of one pilot's knowledge; until there is an answer the field is left out of pilot-made records. *For the owner.* As built, the body a pilot sends (`SiteDraft` in `crates/api-types/src/v1/sites.rs`) has neither `official` nor `discouraged`, nor the record's `url`, which for a source's site is the address of the source's own record.
- Photos come later, as records of their own that point at a site, each with its author and its licence. They need a place for files, which [architecture.md](architecture.md#storage-1) plans ("media under content-hash paths") and nothing builds yet.

Beside the record, what principle 2 asks: an identifier of the server's own making that is never reused (`venti:` and 26 characters, as flights and groups have); `created_at` and the author; `changed_at` and who changed it last; a revision number; the licence, said once for the layer.

### Who may add, who may correct, history and undo

Proposed, as OpenStreetMap does it and for the same reason, that a site outlives its first author:

- **Any signed-in pilot may add** a site, and **any signed-in pilot may correct** one. *For the owner* (the other way: only the author and the operator change a record, others propose). Built as proposed, with the rule in one function (`may_write`, `crates/server/src/pilot_sites.rs`).
- **Every change is a revision that is kept:** a table of revisions (site, number, author, time, what came in through, the whole record as it then stood, a line saying why) beside the table of sites as they stand. Nothing is overwritten.
- **Undo is a new revision** that restores an earlier state and names it. **Removing is a revision too:** the site is marked as gone and stays answerable by its identifier as gone, so that those who mirror learn of it (principle 9).
- **Bounds per account**, ours and to set: so many new sites and so many changes a day; an account whose publishing is suspended (`accounts.publishing_suspended_at`) writes none. Built with 20 new sites and 100 revisions a day (`Limits` in `crates/server/src/pilot_sites.rs`); both numbers are estimates.
- **An account that is deleted** takes its flights with it today (`ON DELETE CASCADE` on every table of `0002` to `0010`). A site given under an open licence cannot go that way without tearing holes into what others built on it. Proposed: the site and its history stay, and the revisions lose their author. The terms must say so before the first one is given. *For the owner.* Built as proposed (`ON DELETE SET NULL` on the three columns that name an account, `0013`).

### Beside OpenStreetMap's sites, never in one record

The answer of `GET /v1/sites` is already a list of layers, one per source, each with its own credit and licence (`SiteLayer` in `crates/api-types/src/v1/sites.rs`), and the core keeps kept sites in one file per source. Venti's own sites are **one more layer** with a source of its own (`venti` in what follows), never mixed into OpenStreetMap's.

- A pilot who corrects a site that is OpenStreetMap's is sent to OpenStreetMap, as today (every record carries its element's address). Venti does not keep corrections to another source's record.
- Where both layers have the same take-off, both are handed out. A pilot-made site may **name** the OpenStreetMap element it is the same place as (an identifier, no copied field: "Resolution links their records to others but does not copy fields across", [data-sources.md](data-sources.md#licence-rules-built-into-the-pipeline)); not built. Which of two is shown is, since 2026-11-22, the server's rule, as `same_place.rs` is for stations: `GET /v1/sites` shows the record of the partners' layer and names the other source's in `same_place`, by identifier only (`crates/server/src/api/site_places.rs`; the decision log in [architecture.md](architecture.md), 2026-11-22). The crowdsourced layer comes after every source. *To verify:* that storing another database's identifier beside a record makes nothing a derived database.
- **A pilot-made site must come from the pilot's own knowledge**: their own position, their own flights, what they see on the hill. An app must not offer "copy this OpenStreetMap site into Venti", and the terms must rule out copying from OpenStreetMap, the DHV's database, ParaglidingEarth or a printed guide. Otherwise the layer's licence is a promise Venti cannot keep.
- *To verify before an app lets a pilot set the pin on the map:* whether a point placed by eye over a map drawn from OpenStreetMap, or over an orthophoto under CC BY 4.0, is free of those works' licences. The OpenStreetMap Foundation has guidelines on this (trivial and horizontal layers); they were not read for this review. A position from the phone's own GPS, or from where the pilot's own flights began, has no such question.

### The licence: CC0 1.0, decided by the owner

**Decided on 2026-11-21.** Asked under which licence pilots give their take-offs to Venti, the owner answered: "CC0, public domain". A site a pilot makes on Venti, with everything in its record and the description among it, and every revision of it, is given under CC0 1.0 Universal. The code names it in one place (`LICENCE` in `crates/server/src/pilot_sites.rs`), from which every answer that carries such a record takes its `attributions`.

**What CC0 1.0 says**, from its deed at <https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/>, read on 2026-11-21: whoever puts a work under it has dedicated the work to the public domain, by waiving every right they have in it under copyright law anywhere in the world, related and neighbouring rights included, as far as the law allows. Anyone may then copy, change, distribute and perform the work, for commercial ends too, "all without asking permission". The deed adds what it leaves alone: nobody's patent or trademark rights are affected; rights other people may have in the work or in its use, such as publicity or privacy rights, are not affected; whoever gave the work gives no warranty and takes no liability, as far as the law allows; and whoever uses the work must not suggest that its author endorses them. The deed is a summary. The legal code it stands for is at <https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode> and was not read: *to verify* there, before the terms are written, what happens where a country's law does not let a person give up their rights, and that the waiver covers the rights in a database.

What the choice had to serve: handing the community's data out freely (decision 14), keeping it apart from other families (principle 4), and the way back into OpenStreetMap. The three that were weighed, as the review laid them out. Of CC BY 4.0 and ODbL 1.0 no text was read; every statement about those two, and about what the OpenStreetMap Foundation takes, is *to verify*.

| | CC0 1.0, **decided** | CC BY 4.0 | ODbL 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| What a reader must do | Nothing | Name the source | Name the source; pass on a changed or combined database under ODbL |
| Handed out freely | Most: any use, any combination | Yes, with a credit line that every later user must carry | With strings: whoever builds a database on it must open theirs. That keeps a closed app from taking without giving, and keeps some open ones from taking at all |
| The same family as OpenStreetMap's sites | No; stays a layer of its own | No; stays a layer of its own | The same licence. The rule would still keep the two apart as sources, but nothing in the licence would forbid one bundle |
| Into OpenStreetMap | Yes, by anyone, with nothing to ask | Only with a written waiver, by what the OpenStreetMap Foundation has said of CC BY | Not simply: OpenStreetMap takes contributions under its own contributor terms, and data that is merely ODbL from a third party is an import to be agreed, case by case |
| A site's name beside a flight or in a plan | Free. This answers the two questions [plan.md](plan.md#needed-from-the-owner) has open about OpenStreetMap's names ("Public flights" (2), "Groups and plans" (10)) for every site that is in Venti's own layer | Free with the credit | Open again: the same share-alike question as today |
| Credit to pilots | None owed; Venti still shows who made each revision | Owed, to "Venti contributors" or by name | Owed, to the database |

**Why CC0.** It is the only one of the three that lets the data go anywhere, OpenStreetMap included, and it removes the question of a site's name beside a flight. What it costs: anyone, a commercial app too, may take the whole layer and give nothing back, and a pilot is owed no credit outside Venti.

**What the decision leaves.**

- **Who holds the rights** was a second question under the first: whether each pilot licenses their contribution directly to the world, or grants Venti a broad licence and Venti publishes the collection. With CC0 it nearly disappears: the pilot gives up their rights, and there is nothing left for Venti to hold or to change later. The terms still have to say who "Venti" is. *For the owner.*
- **A dedication is for good.** The deed's waiver has no way back, so a site and its revisions stay when their pilot leaves, without the pilot's name. That is built ([above](#what-stands-of-it-2026-11-21)) and has to be said to the pilot before the first site is given. Until the [terms for contributors](#what-the-terms-for-contributors-must-say) say it, a server takes no site from a pilot unless its operator set `sites_from_pilots`.
- **CC0 is the pilot's promise about their own rights and says nothing of anyone else's.** The deed leaves the rights of others untouched: a site copied from OpenStreetMap, from the DHV's database or from a guide is not made free by the pilot's dedication. The rule that a site comes from the pilot's own knowledge ([above](#beside-openstreetmaps-sites-never-in-one-record)) carries the layer's licence, and the terms have to state it.
- **The credit line** that travels with the layer is "Venti contributors". CC0 asks for none; it says where the data is from.
- **Photos**, when they come, are creative works and want their own choice (CC BY 4.0 is the usual one). Not decided.

### Moderation

What exists carries over: a report is a row with a kind and a subject (`reports`, `crates/core/src/report.rs`), and the operator acts by command. Needed: a kind `site` among the report's kinds (a migration of the constraint, as `0012` did for groups), reasons that fit a place (does not exist, wrong place, private ground or closed, offensive text, copied from elsewhere), and commands `site` (the record with its history), `revert-site` and `remove-site`. The three commands are built (2026-11-21); the report's kind is not, because a report is about an account that exists and a site may have none ([above](#what-stands-of-it-2026-11-21)). A history that anyone can read and any pilot can undo is the first line; the operator is the second. What a site says about whether one may fly there is the hard part and no code decides it: every pilot-made site is to be shown as "added by pilots, not verified", as OpenStreetMap's are shown as not being permission to fly (`crates/core/src/sites.rs`).

### What flights already say about a take-off

- The server has the place every stored flight began, as a point with a spatial index for the public ones (`flights.takeoff`, `flights_public_takeoff_idx`, `0008`), and its height (`takeoff_altitude_m`).
- `GET /v1/flights/public?bbox=` already answers "the flights from here", and the web app has a page for a place built on it ([architecture.md](architecture.md#everyones-flights-in-the-browser)).
- The core counts a pilot's own logbook by take-off (`site_statistics` in `crates/core/src/statistics.rs`), naming a place by the pilot's own waypoints.

What follows for the design: (1) a site's page can say how many public flights began there, in which months, how far they went, from the server's own data, with no licence question once the site is Venti's; (2) places where the public flights of several pilots began and no site of any layer stands are **candidates**, offered to a pilot who flew there as "add this take-off", with the position and height from their own flight; never added by the server itself, and never from private or unlisted flights; (3) a flight could name the Venti site it began at, by identifier.

## Tokens: what stands (2026-11-21)

Piece 3, on the server. [api.md](api.md#tokens-for-other-programs) has it for the maker of another program, [architecture.md](architecture.md#tokens-for-other-programs) how it is built and why.

**What was run.** The store's suite against the in-memory store and against PostgreSQL 16 with PostGIS in a container, the migration `0014` with it (`crates/store/tests/suite/accounts.rs`, `pilot_sites.rs`); the API through the real router (`crates/server/tests/tokens.rs`), which has the script the build order names: the development mode's token for flights uploads a flight and is refused the groups, the pilot withdraws it, and its next request is `401`.

- **The token.** Made by a signed-in pilot with a name of up to 60 characters, at least one scope, an end if wanted, and the password. The secret is `venti_pat_` and 256 random bits, shown once; the server keeps a hash. Listed with its name, scopes, when it was made, the day it was last used, and its end. Withdrawn at any time, which holds at the next request. 20 per account.
- **The scopes**, eight when the piece was built: `account:read`, `flights:read`, `flights:write`, `live:write`, `sites:write`, `groups:read`, `groups:write`, `reports:write`; ten since the kept take-offs came to the account on the same day, with `kept-sites:read` and `kept-sites:write`. The table of which request needs which is in api.md, and a test holds it to the code.
- **One place that checks.** A handler says what it asks of the caller in its signature and cannot be written without (`May`, `Viewing`, `OwnSession` in `crates/server/src/api/auth.rs`). A session may do everything.
- **What no token may do:** see or end sessions, change the password, delete the account, make, list or withdraw tokens.
- **Origin** (principle 2). A revision of a pilot-made site that came through a token says so, and the store keeps the token's name with it. Tables that keep no origin and should, each a column and a migration: `flights` (the upload, and who changed its visibility through what), `live_tracks`, `groups`, `group_plans` with `group_plan_pilots`, `reports`.
- **Bounds** (principle 12). A token shares every bound of its account; none is per token. Piece 11. Since 2026-11-21 a token for a program also has a budget of requests of its own, said in headers ([api.md](api.md#limits)).
- **The development mode** starts with one token of the demo account, "Demo logger", for reading and writing flights; its secret is in api.md as the demo password is.

- **In the core** (2026-11-21, `crates/core/src/tokens.rs`): the scopes in words for a pilot, the form's words, the check of a name before sending, the requests to make, list and withdraw a token, the reader of the answer with the secret and the words for the one moment it is shown, the words that ask before a withdrawal, and every refusal in words; the wasm binding carries all of it. Tested against the server's golden answers; on no screen yet ([design.md](design.md#account-and-sharing), "Programs with access").

Not built in this piece: the apps' screens for making, listing and withdrawing a token; a command for whoever runs the server to list or withdraw an account's tokens. ## Kept take-offs on the account: what stands (2026-11-21)

Of piece 7 the kept take-offs, on the server and in the core. Waypoints and routes are not begun. [api.md](api.md#the-sites-a-pilot-keeps) has it for a program.

**What was run.** The store's suite against the in-memory store and against PostgreSQL 16 with PostGIS in a container, the migration `0016` with it (`crates/store/tests/suite/kept_sites.rs`); the API through the real router with golden answers that api.md shows (`crates/server/tests/kept_sites.rs`); the core against those answers and against answers made up for the tests (`crates/core/src/kept_sync.rs`). No app was built with it, and no two devices were put in step through a running server: *to verify*.

- **The list on the account.** A signed-in pilot's kept sites, each under the site's own identifier with its source: a copy of the record as it was when kept, with the source's credit and the date of the source's data, and when it was kept (`0016_kept_sites.sql`, `crates/store/src/kept_site.rs`). It is private (principle 5: there is one value, the account, and so no column; no request reads it for anybody else) and goes with the account (`ON DELETE CASCADE`): it is the pilot's own list of other sources' records, and no contribution.
- **In step as flights are** (principle 6). A change number per account, `GET /v1/account/kept-sites/changes?since=`, with what was forgotten; `PUT /v1/account/kept-sites/{id}` keeps and `DELETE` forgets. Scopes `kept-sites:read` and `kept-sites:write` (principle 8).
- **Bounds** (principle 12). 200 sites per account, 16 kB a body, 500 characters a text of a credit. That a site was forgotten is remembered for the 200 forgotten last; a device whose cursor is older is told everything again (`reset`), so the table of an account cannot grow without end. None of the writes is counted per day: *for the owner* whether it should be.
- **Of two records of one site the one kept later stays**, decided by the server in one function that both stores ask (`kept_from`). A record that comes without a note of when is kept if the account lacks the site and never replaces what the account has.
- **Other sources' records in an account's rows** (principle 4). A row is one source's record with that source beside it and its credit inside it; an answer lists rows of several sources and merges none. Nothing of it is handed out to anyone but the pilot who put it there. *To verify* with the licence rules of [data-sources.md](data-sources.md#licence-rules-built-into-the-pipeline): that a pilot's private copy of an OpenStreetMap record on the server, shown to that pilot with its credit, is use and not redistribution of a derived database.
- **One way in the core.** `Core::keep_site` and `Core::forget_site` are what they were for the apps: they change the device's files. Signed in they also note what the account has yet to learn, and `Core::kept_sites_sync` carries it there and brings back what the pilot's other devices did, in the logbook's steps (`SyncStep`). The list's note says where it now lives. **An app has to run that sync**, which no app does yet: until it does, a signed-in pilot's sites wait on the device and the note says so.
- **Signing in** offers the account what the device holds, once: the union. The core reads no clock, so what a device held before it signed in has no note of when and replaces nothing on the account; a site kept while signed in is noted with the time of the sync that sends it. Keeping wins over forgetting where two devices disagree. **Signing out** leaves the device its copy.

Not built: the sync in any app; waypoints and routes on the account; a count of writes per day; through what a kept site came in (principle 2).

## Public flights as data: what stands (2026-11-21)

Piece 5, on the server. [api.md](api.md#following-ventis-public-flights) has it for the maker of another program, [architecture.md](architecture.md#flights) how it is built and why.

**What was run.** The store's suite against the in-memory store and against PostgreSQL 16 with PostGIS in a container, the migration `0017` with it, also over a database that held public flights from before (`crates/store/tests/suite/flights.rs`, `crates/store/tests/postgres.rs`); the API through the real router (`crates/server/tests/public_flights.rs`), which has the script the build order names: a copy that follows the changes holds exactly the public flights, a day after flights were made private, shared by link only, deleted and lost with their account.

- **The moment a flight became public** is `published_at`, on the flight and in every answer that shows a public flight. It is since when the flight is public without a break: withdrawn and public again, it has the moment of the second time. The flights that were public before `0017` have their `uploaded_at`, the earliest they can have been public: the table had kept nothing nearer.
- **One count of changes for what the public sees**, across all accounts: making a flight public, working a public flight out again, and withdrawing one. A flight is **withdrawn** by whatever takes it from the public: its pilot makes it private or shares it by link only, whoever runs the server takes it down or suspends the account's publishing, the pilot deletes it, or the account goes. Each leaves a row that outlives the flight.
- **Reading.** `GET /v1/flights/public` by pilot, by the box the flight took off in, by the span of time it started in, and the three together, newest first or best first. `GET /v1/flights/public/changes?since=`, of everyone's flights or of one pilot's: the flights that became public or changed, and those withdrawn. A flight shared by link is in none of it.
- **What "place" a flight has:** where it took off, a point with an index for the public ones (`flights.takeoff`, `0008`). The landing is in the record and cannot be asked by. Where a flight went is its track.
- **A withdrawn flight is named by the hash of its address.** A flight shared by link is read by whoever has its address, so the changes do not hand out the addresses of flights that were just taken off the public list. A program that kept the flight finds it by the hash.
- **The track as data:** `GET /v1/flights/{id}/track`, a GeoJSON Feature with a LineString: every fix of the recording as longitude, latitude and satellite altitude, with the seconds since the first fix and the pressure altitudes as lists beside it, and where the analysis found the take-off and the landing. At most 50,000 positions, thinned evenly beyond that: a recording of more than thirteen hours at a fix a second. The flight's summary as JSON is `GET /v1/flights/{id}`, as before.
- **The terms in one place.** `FlightTerms::current` in `crates/api-types/src/v1/flights.rs`, carried as `terms` by every answer that holds a flight somebody other than its pilot may read. **Not decided:** it says that the flights are their pilots', shown for reading, and that reuse needs the pilot's leave until Venti's terms say otherwise; it names no licence, and says of itself that it is not decided (`"decided": false`). [For the owner](#for-the-owner), 5.
- **Bounds** (principle 12): 600 reads an hour per address of the list, the changes and tracks together, counted as the forecast's bound is (`crates/server/src/accounts/throttle.rs`); 100 flights or changes a page; 50,000 positions a track.
- **The development mode** has eleven public flights to follow and one that was withdrawn (five since this piece, six more since 2026-11-21 from `dev::add_demo_takeoffs`).

Not built in this piece:

- **The core and the apps** read none of it. They read the answers they read before, which gained fields and lost none.
- **An account's deletion says nothing of when**: its flights are withdrawn without a time (`Store::delete_account` is not told the time).
- **A withdrawal is told for good.** Nothing removes a row of `flight_withdrawals` but the flight's return; how long one must be told is for the terms.
- **A file of all public flights** (piece 10 leaves flights out), webhooks, a bound per token (piece 11), origin on a flight (which app or token uploaded it or changed who sees it).
- **The pilot's public identifier is still the handle** (works-against point 8): a program that follows one pilot follows a handle.

## A description for programs, and open reads: what stands (2026-11-21)

Piece 4, on the server. [api.md](api.md#writing-a-program-against-venti) has a part for the maker of another program and says how to read the description; [architecture.md](architecture.md#the-description-of-the-api) how it is made and why.

**What was run.** The server's tests through the real router, over the development mode's recorded data (`crates/server/tests/openapi.rs`, `crates/server/tests/api.rs`). No client was generated from the document, no OpenAPI tool of another maker read it, and no browser made a request: [Not checked](#not-checked).

- **The description.** `GET /v1/openapi.json`, OpenAPI 3.1 as JSON, and the same document as `docs/openapi.json`. All 75 operations of `/v1` are in it, itself among them, as main stood after the sites' two layers and confirmations came: path, method, path and query parameters, the body, the statuses of success, the answer's schema, the one shape of an error, and who may ask.
- **Who may ask** is said three ways, as `security`: nothing; the session; the session or a token with one named scope. The scopes are `Scope`'s own names, and a test holds each operation's to the table in api.md.
- **The schemas**: 151, one for every record that goes over the wire, named as the Rust type. No schema closes a record against further fields. A list of values that may grow (error codes, scopes, kinds with an "unknown" in the type) is `x-venti-values`; one that is whole is `enum`.
- **The licence statement** (principle 3) is in the schemas with what it asks: `Attribution`, which every answer with a source's data carries as `attributions`, and `FlightTerms`, which answers with pilots' flights carry as `terms`.
- **Paging** (principle 6) is in the parameters, in words: `after` with an answer's `next`, `since` with an answer's `cursor`, `more` and `reset`.
- **What holds it true**: tests that every route of the router is described and the reverse, that every golden answer is valid against the schema of its operation with no field the schema lacks, that what the description calls open is answered without a token and nothing else is, and that the served document is the file.
- **Reads open to every origin.** A `GET` or `HEAD` under `/v1` without an `Authorization` header is answered `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, with `ETag`, `Retry-After`, `Accept-Ranges` and `Content-Range` readable; a preflight for such a read is answered for `GET` and `HEAD` with `If-None-Match`, `Range` and `If-Range` and nothing else. No setting turns it off.
- **What stayed.** A request with a token, with a body, or with a method that writes is for the origins of `cors_origins`, as before. `Access-Control-Allow-Credentials` is never set. The session is a token in the `Authorization` header that the page's own code sends; the server sets and reads no cookie, so a page of another site has nothing of a visitor's to send. Venti's own web app asks the origin it is served from and needs no entry in `cors_origins` unless it is served from another.

Not built in this piece, and what the description does not hold:

- **No client of another maker.** The "done when" of the build order asks for a client generated in another language and a page on another origin; neither was made.
- **Which error an operation answers.** Every operation names the one error shape, and the codes are a list at the schema; which status and code come when is api.md's table only.
- **The parts nothing checks**: fields the recorded data never shows, fourteen schemas no golden file reaches (the bodies of six requests among them), the query parameters, the statuses of success, and that the list of error codes is complete. [architecture.md](architecture.md#the-description-of-the-api) lists them.
- **The schemas are loose where the types are**: a record that is one of several shapes (what a report is about, a site's change) is described as an object whose fields may each be absent; `DayTime` of an airspace's hours has no schema but "anything".
- **The connector and the pages for people** are not under `/v1` and not in it; the connector describes itself by its protocol ([mcp.md](mcp.md)).
- **A bound on open reads from browsers** beyond what every address has (piece 11).

### What the terms for contributors must say

Named here, not written: under which licence each kind of contribution is given and handed out (sites under CC0 1.0, decided 2026-11-21; later photos, notes), and that it is given for good: a site and its history stay when the account goes, without the pilot's name; that a pilot gives only what is theirs to give, from their own knowledge and flights, and copies from no map, database or guide; what happens to each kind when the account is deleted, and that openly licensed contributions stay without their author's name; who can see what (the values of each visibility); under what terms others may reuse a flight a pilot made public; that another maker's app acts for a pilot only with a token the pilot gave and can withdraw; that whoever runs Venti may remove a contribution and on what grounds; that nothing on Venti is permission to fly anywhere; who is responsible for the data, by name and with an address, as data protection law asks (*to verify* what exactly: tracks and positions are personal data).

## What in today's server works against it

In the order of how much it would hurt to leave.

1. **One credential that does everything, got with the password** (`crates/server/src/api/auth.rs`). No other maker's app can act for a pilot without the pilot's password, and no token can be limited. Every endpoint added before scopes exist is one more to sort later. Piece 3. Answered on 2026-11-21: [tokens with scopes](#tokens-what-stands-2026-11-21), and a handler that names no scope does not compile. What is left of it is that the pilot copies a secret by hand until piece 12.
2. **Sites are a source's whole set, replaced with each run** (`crates/store/src/site.rs`, `0005`): a record is text, without author, time or history, and goes when its set is replaced. A pilot-made site cannot live in that table. The answer's shape, layers by source, is right and stays. Answered on 2026-11-21 by piece 1: pilots' sites have tables of their own (`0013`, `crates/store/src/pilot_site.rs`) and are one more layer of the same answer; a source's sites are as they were, and get an address that promises only what a whole set can ([`lasting`](#reading)).
3. **A flight has no life as public data.** No moment it became public, no terms for reuse, no change number across accounts, no way to learn that a flight is public no more except by asking for it, and the list is ordered by the flight's start only (`0002`, `0008`, `crates/server/src/api/feed.rs`). A mirror cannot be kept true, so anyone who copies public flights will keep flights their pilots withdrew. Piece 5. Answered on 2026-11-21: [public flights as data](#public-flights-as-data-what-stands-2026-11-21). A flight has the moment it became public, there is one count of changes across accounts, a withdrawal is recorded and told, and the list is asked by pilot too. What is left of it is the terms: every answer names them, and they are a holding statement until the owner decides.
4. **Everything a pilot made goes with the account** (`ON DELETE CASCADE` on `account_id` throughout). Right for flights and plans; wrong for data given to everyone. The rule has to be chosen per kind before the first such kind exists. For sites it is chosen as recommended, the owner's to confirm: they stay, without their author (`0013`).
5. **Browsers of others are shut out of public data** (`cors_origins`). Piece 4. Answered on 2026-11-21: what anyone may read is answered to the pages of every origin; what takes a token or writes is as it was.
6. **The documented API is prose and a Rust crate.** [api.md](api.md) is exact and tested, and 4,300 lines long; a maker in another language writes every type by hand. Piece 4. Answered on 2026-11-21: the server hands out an OpenAPI document that tests hold to it. What is left of it is that the schemas are kept by hand beside the Rust types, and that no client of another maker has been generated from them.
7. **Answers shaped for one screen with no plain answer beside them.** `GET /v1/stations` leaves out one of two stations at the same place and offers no way to ask for both (`same_place.rs`); a flight's `outline` is "for a thumbnail" and the track as data is the IGC file only; the ranking holds its rule as a constant (`RANKING_COUNTED`); the public list knows a flight by where it began and not where it went. Each is right for the apps; a platform also gives the unjudged data.
8. **The pilot has one public identifier, the handle**, and changing a handle is on the list of what is to come ([api.md](api.md#what-is-not-here-yet)). The account's own identifier is never handed out. Before handles can change, either they are declared permanent or a pilot gets an identifier that is. *For the owner.*
9. **Bounds are per address or per account, and in the process's memory** (`crates/server/src/accounts/throttle.rs`, [mcp.md](mcp.md#what-a-request-may-cost)), and most reads have none. One address with many pilots behind it (a club's app, an assistant's provider) shares one budget; nothing tells a good client from a flood. Piece 11.
10. **A new kind of community data costs a migration of the reports' constraint** and a new form in the core (`0012`, `crates/core/src/report.rs`). Bearable; said so that the next kind plans for it. The first such kind showed a second cost: a report is about an account, and a site can be without one, which is why piece 1 has no reports yet.
11. **The pilot's own places are on the device by decision** (`kept_sites.rs`, `waypoints.rs`, `task.rs`). Piece 7. Answered for the kept take-offs on 2026-11-21, in the server and the core; waypoints and the task are as they were.

## For the owner

Decided on 2026-11-21 and no longer in this list: the licence of pilot-made sites, [CC0 1.0](#the-licence-cc0-10-decided-by-the-owner).

1. **Who may correct a site.** Settled by the owner on 2026-11-21: a partner's site, partners only; a crowdsourced site, any signed-in pilot and any partner, with every change kept and undoable. Built so; the rule is one function (`may_write`, `crates/server/src/pilot_sites.rs`). What it opens: **how a partner is chosen**, and **what a partner agrees to** before the first site (that it is given under CC0 1.0, shown with the handle, and stays when the trust or the account goes). Neither is written anywhere.
2. **What an account's deletion leaves of open contributions.** Recommended: the sites and their history stay, without the author. Built so (`0013`); with CC0 the dedication cannot be taken back in any case, so what is left to decide is whether the server goes on showing them.
3. **Whether a pilot may mark a site as official.** Recommended: no; leave the field to a later answer about clubs. Built so: the body a pilot sends has no such field (`SiteDraft`).
4. **Which of two layers a map draws where both have the same take-off.** Recommended: Venti's own, with OpenStreetMap's reachable from its page. Not built: both are drawn.
5. **Under what terms a public flight may be reused by others.** Recommended: readable by anyone as today; reuse with the pilot's name (CC BY 4.0) for flights made public after the terms exist; no file of all public flights, because a track is personal data and a file outlives a withdrawal. Until decided, every answer says in one wording (`FlightTerms::current`) that the flights are their pilots' and that reuse needs the pilot's leave; `published_at` is kept so that "made public after the terms exist" can be told. With it, **the numbers of piece 5**, all estimates: 600 reads an hour per address, 50,000 positions a track; **whether a withdrawn flight is named by the hash of its address**, built so; and **how long a copy may lag behind a withdrawal**, which the document for makers gives as a day until the terms say.
6. **Whether a handle is permanent.** Recommended: yes, until a second, lasting identifier for a pilot exists. A site's history now names its pilots by handle, which makes the question more pressing.
7. **Whether the server works out a pilot's statistics and "How you fly" from the files it holds.** Recommended: yes for the comparison with others, from public flights only; a pilot's own figures stay the core's.
8. **Who "Venti" is in the terms**, and the terms themselves. Until they exist, no public server should take sites from pilots (`sites_from_pilots` stays off).
9. **The numbers of piece 1**, all estimates: 20 m between two sites, 20 new sites and 100 revisions a day per account, 200 characters for a revision's line; and of confirmations: a day up to 90 days back, a flight's take-off within 500 m of the site, 50 a day per account, the newest 100 listed.
9a. **Confirmations**: under what terms a mirror may keep a pilot's name and note (they are not under CC0); whether a flight's address should be kept with a confirmation, to link a public flight; whether a confirmation can be reported by itself.
9b. **A partner's site where a pilot's stands**: both stand today. Whether a partner or the operator moves the pilot's one into the partners' layer ("adopt") or it is removed.
10. **The cut of piece 3**, built as recommended and the owner's to confirm: whether `groups:write` takes in the keeper's acts (deleting a group, handing it over, a new invite code); whether a program may send reports for a pilot (`reports:write`); whether the public history of a site should say which token a revision came through, where it now says only that it was one; the prefix `venti_pat_`; 20 tokens per account and 60 characters for a name; that making a token asks for the password.

12. **The cut of piece 4**, built as seemed right and the owner's to confirm: that reads without a token are open to the pages of every origin on every server, with no setting to close them; what api.md promises of `/v1` under "What is promised, and what is not" (a path, a field's name, type and meaning, an error's code and an identifier stay; the bounds, the caching and the wording of messages may change; nothing is said of how long `/v1` would stay beside a `/v2`); that the description lists the error codes without saying which operation answers which; the names generated clients get for operations (`operationId`, made from method and path).

11. **The cut of the kept take-offs on the account**, built as seemed right and the owner's to confirm: 200 sites per account; that signing in joins the device's list and the account's (and so a device two pilots use hands the first pilot's sites to the second pilot's account); that a site kept on one device and forgotten on another stays kept; that signing out leaves the list on the device; that the note says "Kept on this device, and on your Venti account after the next sync." while something waits; that a program may read and write the list with scopes of its own.

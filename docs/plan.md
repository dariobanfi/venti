# Product plan

Last revised: 2026-11-21

## Goal

Paragliding pilots use one app to check conditions, another to plan, another as flight computer, another to publish flights, and chat groups to organise flying days. None of them share data. This project puts those jobs into one free app with one community, so that a flight is planned, flown, analysed and shared in the same place.

## Owner decisions

These were set by the owner on 2026-11-18 and are not open for re-litigation in later sessions.

1. **Free and open source.** No monetisation planned. Sponsorship may come later.
2. **The project does not pay for AI.** Users connect their own assistant or credentials.
3. **Native UI on every platform.** SwiftUI on iOS, Jetpack Compose on Android, a real web app in the browser. No React Native, no Flutter, no web views inside the mobile apps.
4. **One Rust codebase** below the UI, shared by all clients and the server.
5. **One central platform, self-hosted on one Linux server** the owner pays for, and on a set of them when it grows. No services that bill by usage. Venti is not made for clubs or pilots to run a server of their own: there is one Venti. Until it has an address, the apps ask for a server's address in their settings, which is a means of development: with the address known it is built into the apps, the setting is for developers, and the words that speak of "your Venti server" say "Venti".
6. **Every external data source sits behind an adapter.** Regions are configuration. See [data-sources.md](data-sources.md).
7. **First region: Germany and Austria**, then the whole Alps. The owner is based in Munich.
8. **iOS first.** The owner flies with an iPhone and an external Bluetooth vario.
9. **XCTrack is the benchmark for the cockpit.** See [xctrack-parity.md](xctrack-parity.md).
10. **Best available open maps, with very good terrain depiction.**
11. **AI sessions write and maintain all code.** The repository must stay understandable to a session that has never seen it.
12. **The app is called Venti** and is licensed under AGPL-3.0-or-later.
13. **It must be able to carry many users.** The server design starts on one machine and must be able to grow beyond it.

## Modes

The app has five modes on web and phone. The cockpit is phone-only.

| Mode | Job |
|---|---|
| **Today** | What is flyable right now: live wind, webcams, who is in the air |
| **Plan** | Forecast and route planning for cross-country and hike & fly |
| **Fly** | Live cockpit and flight recorder |
| **Review** | Logbook, 3D replay, insights, comparison with other pilots |
| **Community** | Feed, groups, events, spontaneous flying days |

All modes read and write the same objects through one API: sites, wind stations, airspace, flights, pilots, live positions. The assistant uses that same API as its tools.

## Features

This list is a draft. The owner has said more feature input will follow.

### Today and Plan
- Live wind stations from every usable network on one map, with history graphs.
- Forecast layers with a time slider: wind at several altitudes, thermal strength, cloud base, cloud cover, rain, live radar.
- Takeoffs and landings with allowed wind directions, a per-site flyability timeline, webcams, rules and recent flights.
- Airspace with altitude and time filters; cable and obstacle hazards.
- Thermal hotspots and common cross-country lines.
- Lifts, public transport stops and hiking trails.
- **An aerial view of the map** (the owner, 2026-11-20: "very important"): the map switched to photographs from above, in every app, with what Venti draws over it left in place. Built on the web, the iPhone and Android, at the round layers button of the map tab and of a flight's page (on the iPhone and Android also of the route planner), one choice for every map. The photographs are a 10 m mosaic of the whole world and, zoomed in, Bavaria's 20 cm orthophoto and Austria's at 30 cm, which Venti's server cuts at the border ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md#the-aerial-views-photographs)). The cockpits of the iPhone and Android have it too since 2026-11-21, on their map page and flight page, with the photographs from the network only. To do: Switzerland and South Tyrol; photographs kept on the device for flying without a network.
- **The forecast is built on ICON-D2** (the owner, 2026-11-20), DWD's 2.2 km model, which is also what Burnair's forecasts are made from. It is what Venti reads already; see [data-sources.md](data-sources.md#forecasts).
- **A thermal forecast, and the kind of day it makes** (the owner, 2026-11-21: "the most useful thing about the burnair app is the thermal forecast", a day rated from a super day through a cross-country day and a day to fly on to a day not to fly). Built in the core on 2026-11-21 as Venti's own estimate from ICON-D2: how high thermals reach, the cloud base, the climb to expect, and the kind of day by fixed limits (big day, cross-country day, local thermals, weak thermals; a day that is not flyable says nothing of thermals). Method, limits and what is weak in it: [design.md](design.md#thermals). The kind of day on the map, region by region, as Burnair shows it: the server's side is built on 2026-11-21, a map of 0.1° cells over the regions made with every run of ICON-D2 by the same rules and served as one answer (`GET /v1/thermals`, [api.md](api.md#get-v1thermals)); since the same day the day after tomorrow on the map is ICON-EU's, each day naming its model, and what the map judged is kept across a restart of the server. Shown since the same day on the three apps' Today screens and take-off pages, with a picture of the day's air hour by hour, and in the web's forecast table; a take-off's later days are read from ICON-EU where ICON-D2 ends. The map's layer ("Forecast") is in the three apps, with what speaks against a day and the way from a square to the forecast for its place; the forecast table's two thermal rows are on all three. To do: colours of their own for the kinds of day and for the day that is not flyable in the design pack, which is the owner's (the map stands in with steps of the thermal scale and a veil); the estimate checked against flown climbs, which the server could do from the flights it holds; and DWD's raw model levels and mixing height for a finer profile.
- Later: cross-country route planner, hike & fly planner (trail up, glide range down, transit home), alerts such as "tell me when Wallberg is flyable".

### Fly
The full checklist is in [xctrack-parity.md](xctrack-parity.md). XCTrack is Android-only and its developers state that they do not plan an iOS version, so an iPhone cockpit at that level does not exist today. The principles:
- The recorder runs as a native background service. A UI crash or phone call never costs the track.
- **The flight starts by itself** (asked for by the owner on 2026-11-20; on the iPhone since that day, Android to follow). As XCTrack does it: the pilot opens the cockpit on the ground, and it is ready ("Ready for take-off"). It keeps the last half minute of positions and writes nothing; when the flight computer notices the take-off (4 m/s over the ground for 5 s, or 25 m of height steadily over half a minute), the recording begins from that half minute and live tracking starts; two minutes after the glider has come to rest the flight is saved to the logbook without a tap. Being ready costs battery, so the cockpit closes by itself after two hours without a take-off. "Record now" records at once. The rules are in the core (`core::cockpit`, `flight::live`); all of them are to verify in the air.
- Everything works offline: maps, terrain, airspace, sites and the last forecast are on the device.
- External Bluetooth varios are first-class, because phone barometers are too slow for a good vario.
- Safety-relevant warnings (airspace, terrain, landing options) are deterministic code. They never depend on a network connection or a language model.

### Review
- IGC upload and bulk import of existing flight history, with automatic scoring.
- **A pilot's XContest history, brought in** (the owner, 2026-11-20: "think of allowing import from XContest"; and earlier: Venti as a bridge, so that a pilot can use it while still posting to XContest). XContest publishes no way to read a pilot's flights, its flight pages ask for a sign-in and its `robots.txt` shuts them out ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md#leagues-a-flight-goes-to)), so Venti does not read XContest. It does not need to: every flight on XContest began as a file on the pilot's own device, and the pilot still has it.
  - *The files XCTrack keeps* are the whole history of most XContest pilots: one IGC file for every flight, in XCTrack's tracklog folder on the phone and in whatever backup the pilot keeps of it. The owner's 45 flights of four years came in from such a folder in 13 seconds on 2026-11-20, each read, analysed and scored. So the import is: point Venti at a folder once, and everything in it is in the logbook. A flight the logbook already has is recognised by its positions and not taken twice, so the same folder can be brought in again at any time.
  - *The bridge, built on Android on 2026-11-20:* the logbook's plus button has "Bring in new flights from a folder"; Venti keeps Android's permission to read the folder the pilot chose and looks into it whenever it comes to the front, bringing in the flight files that are new or changed (a file is known by where it lies, its size and when it changed; the core recognises a flight it has in any case). A pilot then flies with XCTrack as before, the flight is in Venti's logbook by itself, and from there it goes to a league with "Send to a league". *Where XCTrack keeps its tracklogs, to verify on a phone:* by what is known here, current XCTrack writes them to its own folder under `Android/data/org.xcontest.XCTrack/`, and since Android 11 the system's folder picker does not offer `Android/data` to another app at all; Venti cannot be given that folder, and no app can without broad storage rights, which Venti does not ask for. If so, the way is XCTrack's own setting for where tracklogs go (*to verify that it has one, and what it offers*), pointed at a folder both apps can see, such as one under Documents; or a copy the pilot keeps elsewhere (a cloud folder that the system's picker shows). Untried on the emulator, which has no XCTrack: the permission kept across a restart of the phone, a folder on a cloud provider, and a folder of thousands of flights.
  - *One flight at a time, from XContest itself:* a signed-in pilot can download a flight's file from its page and bring it in like any other (*to verify by the owner, who has an account*). Tedious for a whole history; right for the odd flight that is on no device any more.
  - *Asked of XContest, in the same letter as the upload interface:* a way for a pilot to fetch their own flights, or to export them all at once. A pilot in the EU may ask XContest for their own data in any case; an interface would spare both sides the letters.
  - *Not done:* signing in to XContest inside Venti and reading the pilot's pages with their session, or a script for the pilot's browser that does. Both read pages XContest has shut to programs, and both break whenever a page changes.
- 3D replay, with photos and videos pinned to the moment and place they were taken.
- Automatic breakdown of every thermal and glide, time spent climbing, gliding and searching, wind by altitude.
- Comparison with pilots in the same area that day: climb rate in the same thermals, where lines diverged and what that cost, lift flown past.
- Trends by season, wing and site.
- **Asked for by the owner on 2026-11-20, to do:** a pilot's flying in numbers and what it says about them. (1) Statistics of the flights done, beyond the totals, the months and the bests there are today: by site, by glider, by kind of route, by year against year. Built on 2026-11-21 in the core and shown on the iPhone: year against year, the months of the year over all years, the kinds of route, and the typical flight (the middle one of all); since the same day also on Android and the web. By take-off it is worked out in the core since that day (`site_statistics`: the places flights began at, by the pilot's own waypoint names where there are any) and shown on the iPhone and on Android, the web to follow; by glider there are the totals. (2) The same numbers beside those of the pilots around: the pilots who fly the same sites, the same days, the same class of glider, so that "my average climb" and "my longest flight" have something to stand against. (3) Learnings, tendencies and mistakes, worked out from the pilot's own tracks by the deterministic code that already breaks a flight into thermals and glides: which way they turn, how long they stay in a thermal that has weakened, how high they leave, what their glides cost against the pilots beside them, where flights ended and why, airspace come close to. Said as observations with the flights they are from, never as a verdict on the pilot, and never as advice about what is safe. Built in the core on 2026-11-21 and shown since the same day on the statistics' screen "How you fly" (on the web seen running, on the iPhone and Android written and not yet seen running, as [design.md](design.md#logbook) says): each flight's figures (which way each thermal was circled, how measured thermals ended and the time after their top, height gained, the best thermal, glides of a kilometre) are counted on import and kept with the flight, and `tendencies` says over the whole logbook which way the pilot circles, how their thermals end, the time after the top, how they climb and glide, and the share of the airtime spent circling, each with a flight that shows it; rules and limits in [design.md](design.md#logbook). Since the same day also how far below a thermal's top the pilot leaves it, and where flights end against where they began (within a kilometre, and the farthest). To do: what their glides cost against the pilots beside them; why flights ended where they did (a landing field reached on purpose, or the ground reached first); airspace come close to; and every limit checked with pilots.
- **To do: a flight's times by the clock of the place it was flown at.** A flight's take-off, landing and replay times are shown by the clock of whoever reads them, which is right at home and wrong for a flight flown in another time zone, or looked at from one (found on 2026-11-20 by running real flights through the app). An IGC file says the time in UTC and rarely the zone. Showing the place's own time needs a table of the world's time zones by place and their rules in the core, a few megabytes in every app; the two open tables are timezone-boundary-builder's outlines (ODbL, from OpenStreetMap) and the IANA rules (public domain). To decide: whether that weight is worth it, or whether the page says "your clock" beside the times instead.

### Community
- Profiles, follows, a feed of flights with media, comments, share pages with rich previews.
- Groups with chat and a "flying today?" board, visible to the group or to pilots nearby.
- Events with local discovery; site pages anyone can improve.
- Privacy controls from the first release: private, followers-only or public flights, a hidden home zone, delayed or friends-only live tracking.
- Later: leagues, classic-route leaderboards, club pages. A first ranking exists since 2026-11-20 (the best three public flights of a year). **Asked for by the owner on 2026-11-20, to do:** leaderboards beyond it: by site (its records and its regulars), by kind of route, by class of glider, by region, and among the pilots a pilot follows or flies with.

### Assistant
Users bring their own AI; details are in [architecture.md](architecture.md#bring-your-own-ai).
- **Connector:** the server exposes its tools over MCP, so a pilot adds the app to their own Claude or ChatGPT and asks there ("how is the wind toward Innsbruck this afternoon?").
- **In-app assistant:** runs on the pilot's own API key or account, speaks and listens in flight, and can raise proactive alerts.
- **Flagship question:** "which valley gets me home tonight?" Reachable landings from the current height and wind, then nearest stops, then train and bus itineraries, ranked by arrival time and glide margin.

## Risks

1. **Scope.** This is three mature products at once. The phase order is the mitigation.
2. **Empty community.** XContest has the network. The app must be the best logbook and cockpit with no friends on it, import history in bulk, and cross-post to XContest if XContest agrees. Since 2026-11-20 the iPhone app, and since the same day the Android app, hands a flight to XContest by the pilot's own upload and sends one to the DHV-XC through the interface it publishes ([data-sources.md](data-sources.md#leagues-a-flight-goes-to)); posting to XContest with one tap and fetching a pilot's history from it wait for XContest's answer to the owner.
3. **Lost tracks.** iOS background limits and Android battery managers kill trackers. One lost long flight loses a pilot for good.
4. **Data permissions.** Several sources that matter most at launch sites have no open licence: Holfuy wind stations, the DHV site database, airspace activation times, webcams.
5. **Liability.** Airspace data and assistant answers can be wrong. Everything is advisory, shows the age of its data and cites its source.
6. **Store review.** Background location and background audio need justifying; user content needs report, block and moderation. Since 2026-11-20 the server takes reports and its operator can take things down; the iPhone, the Android and the web app have the form for a report, and nothing blocks. What the members of a group write there, its name and their plans, cannot be reported yet.
